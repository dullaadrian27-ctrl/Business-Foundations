Resource 1.1 · Business Foundations
Mission + Vision Statement Builder
Define what your program stands for, who it serves, and where it's going. These two statements anchor every business decision you make.
Foundation

"You cannot build a scalable business on vibes. Your mission is the filter for every hire, every policy, every family you enroll. If you don't know what you stand for, your team won't either."

— Aliya Johnson-Roberts, AJR Consulting
The difference

Mission is present tense — what you do, who you serve, and why you exist right now. Vision is future tense — where you are going and what the world looks like when you get there. Both are necessary. Neither should be longer than 2–3 sentences.

Step 1 — Know Your Foundation
Step 2 — Draft Your Mission Statement
Mission formula

[Program name] exists to [what you do] for [who you serve] so that [the outcome you create]. We believe [your core belief about children, families, or education].

Step 3 — Draft Your Vision Statement
Vision formula

By [time horizon], [program name] will be [what your program looks like at its best] — a place where [what is true for children / families / staff]. We are building [the legacy / the impact].

Resource 1.3 · Business Foundations
Core Values Workbook
Define the values that drive every hire, every policy, and every family interaction. Your values are your culture. Name them before your culture names itself.
Culture

"Culture is not the poster on the wall. It is what happens when you are not in the room. Your core values are the filter your team uses to make decisions without you."

— Aliya Johnson-Roberts, AJR Consulting
Step 1 — Surface Your Values
Step 2 — Name Your Core Values (3–5 maximum)
How to use this

Each value needs a name AND a one-sentence definition — because "excellence" means something different to every person. Your definition is what you actually mean. Keep it to 3–5 values. More than 5 means none of them are really core.

Systems

"Hiring without a system is hoping. And hope is not a staffing strategy. Before you post one more job, make sure you know what you're hiring for, how you'll evaluate them, and what happens when they start."

— Aliya Johnson-Roberts, AJR Consulting
Before You Post the Job — Pre-Hire Readiness Audit
Check each item that is currently in place

If you are checking less than 70% of these, build the system before you hire. A new person without a system does not solve your staffing problem — it creates a new one.

Your Interview Question Bank
The rule

Always ask behavioral questions — "Tell me about a time when..." not "Would you ever..." What someone has done predicts what they will do. Past behavior is the best interview data you have.
